# tee-pay-v2 design, as implemented

Round 1 as revised on 2026-11-10: the enclave runs behind the bank, serials are one-time Ed25519 public keys derived from a wallet seed, the bank checks the owner signatures itself and forwards the sealed openings to its enclave. SPEC.md is the specification; this file records what the code does, which check carries which property, what the bank sees, and what is still open.

## Parties and processes

| Process | Role | Keys |
|---|---|---|
| `tv-enclave` | checks openings (E1 to E4), signs results | per-boot result key, one X25519 channel key per `Attest` |
| `tv-bank` | the only server users reach; B1 to B4; durable log | state key (user states), issuance key (bank states) |
| `tv-cli` | wallet and flows | 32-byte seed; `sk_i`, `r_i` by HKDF |

The bank's own money lives in an ordinary wallet (`bank-wallet.json`, created by `tv-bank init --reserve N`) whose states are signed with the issuance key. Total supply is the reserve: the bank issues by paying from that wallet, and redeems by being paid into it. Each sealed opening states its side (`Opening.side`, user or bank); the enclave verifies the opening under that side's key only and reports the sides in its result, so the bank signs a bank side's new state with the issuance key again. A user state claiming the bank side, or a bank state claiming the user side, fails E1.

## Primitives (tv-core)

- `sk_i = HKDF-SHA256(seed, salt "tv/v1", info "sk" || i)`, `r_i = HKDF(..., "r" || i)`, `pk_i = Ed25519 public of sk_i`.
- `Com(b, pk, r) = SHA-256("tv/v1/com" || b BE || pk || r)`.
- State signature: `Sign(bank key, "tv/v1/state" || C)`; the state key or the issuance key.
- Digests: `pay = SHA-256("tv/v1/pay" || pk_A || C_A' || pk_B || C_B')`, `refresh = SHA-256("tv/v1/refresh" || pk || C')`, `enroll = SHA-256("tv/v1/enroll" || pk_0 || C_0)`. Owner signatures are Ed25519 over the digest. The amount is in no digest.
- Result signature: `Sign(result key, "tv/v1/result/ok" || n || m || side kinds)`.
- Manifest digest bound in the attestation `user_data`: `SHA-256("tv/v1/manifest" || result pk || state pk || issuance pk)`. Clients and the bank recompute it from the manifest the enclave returns, so an attestation also pins the bank keys the enclave enforces.
- `B_MAX = 1_000_000` (`tv_core::B_MAX`).

## Payment flow

```
1  Bob   -> Bank             Attest(nonce_B, owner = pk_B)
   Bank  -> Enclave          Attest(nonce_B, owner = pk_B)
2  Enclave -> Bank -> Bob    Attestation(doc over nonce_B, channel pk, user_data = tag || manifest digest), manifest
   The enclave remembers (channel pk -> owner) and returns the channel pk to the bank next to the doc; the bank
   counts it against Bob's peer address in its own memory (per-source cap, below). A Check on that key is refused,
   without consuming it, unless the serial it acts for (pk_B of a Receive, pk_0 of an Enroll, pk of a Refresh)
   is the owner.
   Bob verifies the doc, pins (v, C_B' = Com(b_B + v, pk_B+, r_B+), channel pk, result pk) as a pending request,
   writes request.json = (v, pk_B, C_B', attestation bundle)
3  Alice reads request.json, verifies the same doc with Bob's nonce, refuses it unless its timestamp is younger
   than MAX_KEY_AGE and at most ATTEST_CLOCK_SKEW ahead of her clock, checks the manifest names her bank keys,
   builds C_A' = Com(b_A - v, pk_A+, r_A+), m = pay digest, sig_A = Sign(sk_A, m),
   seals (v, b_A, r_A, sigma_A, pk_A+, r_A+) to the channel pk, writes offer.json
4  Bob checks the offer against his pending request, signs m with sk_B, seals his own opening to the same channel pk,
   sends Receive(public = pk_A, C_A', sig_A, pk_B, C_B', sig_B; payer blob; payee blob; channel pk)
5  Bank: B1 answer stored under m? return it; a copy of a request whose m is still in flight waits for that request
         to finish and then re-runs B1 (tv_bank::inflight), so a retried duplicate reads the stored answer instead of
         racing the original to the enclave, where the consumed channel key would refuse it.
         B2 sig_A, sig_B verify on m.  B4a pk_A != pk_B, neither spent.
         n = 32 random bytes; Check(n, Receive) to the enclave over its private link.
   Enclave: look up the channel key for channel pk, open both blobs, then remove the key (one use, only after both blobs authenticated),
         E1 each opening recomputes a commitment signed under the key of the side it states (state key for a user side, issuance key for a bank side),
         E2 C_A' == Com(b_A - v, pk_A+, r_A+) and C_B' == Com(b_B + v, pk_B+, r_B+),
         E3 sig_A and sig_B verify on m,
         E4 0 < v <= b_A, b_B + v <= B_MAX unless Bob's side is a bank side,
         and neither next serial pk_A+, pk_B+ equals pk_A, pk_B or the other next serial (the state would be unspendable);
         every check runs before the outcome is chosen and a refused check still signs a result that is discarded,
         so the time a Check takes does not depend on which check failed;
         answer Result(n, m, [side_A, side_B], signature) or Refused.
   Bank: B3 the result verifies under the registered result key over its own n and this m.
         B4b under the store lock: re-check B1 and the spent set, sign C_A' and C_B' (issuance key for bank sides),
         append one JSON line to log.jsonl, fsync, answer.
6  Bank -> Bob   Settled(sigma_A', sigma_B')   or Refused   or Unavailable (enclave link down)
7  Bob writes receipt.json = (m, pk_A, C_A', sigma_A'); Alice `finish --receipt` or `finish` alone (Lookup(pk_A) in the log).
```

Enroll and refresh follow the same shape with one serial: enroll sends `pk_0, C_0`, the owner signature on the enroll digest and the sealed `r_0`; the enclave checks `Com(0, pk_0, r_0) == C_0`; the bank checks the signature and that `pk_0` and `C_0` are fresh. Refresh sends `pk, C'`, the owner signature on the refresh digest and the sealed opening plus `(pk+, r+)`; the enclave checks E1, that `C'` commits to the same balance and that `pk+ != pk`.

## What each property rests on

- **P1 ownership integrity.** A payment, refresh or burn of the state behind `pk` needs a signature under `pk` on a digest that names the new commitment (and the payee). The bank checks it (B2) before any enclave involvement, so an enclave that skips E3, or an attacker holding every enclave key, still cannot move a state: the owner key is derived from the seed and never leaves the wallet. B1 runs first and serves a settled digest to any sender: the stored answer is the public log entry, so a retried `Receive`, even one with zeroed owner signatures and garbage blobs, is answered `Settled` before B2 runs; that entry exists only because B2 passed on the original. Tests: `p1_ownership.rs`, `leaked_enclave.rs`.
- **P2 balance integrity.** The value equation is checked only where the openings are visible, inside the enclave (E1, E2, E4, checked arithmetic). The bank accepts a result only from its registered enclave key, over a nonce it drew for this call and over the digest it computed itself (B3). Results reach the bank only through its own link to the enclave; no `BankRequest` variant carries a result, and a raw result frame is refused as malformed. Each serial is spent once (B4a before the call, B4b again under the lock before the append). Tests: `p2_balance.rs`; `double_spend.rs` for B4a, including one serial on both sides of a payment under a lenient enclave; `b4b_race.rs` for B4b: two in-flight spends of one serial, two in-flight enrolls of one serial and two of one commitment are held at a delayed enclave and released together, and exactly one of each pair settles.
- **P3 no fund destruction.** Same as P1: a burn is a payment or a refresh, both need the owner signature.
- **P4 privacy toward the bank.** The bank receives serials, new commitments, owner signatures and sealed blobs it cannot open. Its durable state is the public log (kind, consumed serials, new commitments, new signatures, digest) and the registered enclave key; `p4_bank_view.rs` serializes the log file and the in-memory index and checks that no amount, balance, randomness `r`, next serial or seed appears. The consoles are part of the bank's view too: the enclave's log (which the bank host reads) says only `check refused`, never which of E1 to E4 failed, and `p4_bank_view.rs` captures every bank and enclave log line at trace level during refused payments and checks that no refusal variant name and no amount appears. The old commitment `C_A` of the state being spent is not in the request at all: it is only inside the sealed opening, so the bank cannot link `pk_A` to the earlier log entry that published `C_A`. The one serial published next to its commitment is `pk_0`: an `Enroll` entry shows `(pk_0, C_0)` with balance 0 by protocol, so whoever pays `pk_0` (the bank for an issue, which also writes the amount to `issuance.jsonl`) would learn the payee's balance after the payment. `tv-cli enroll` therefore enrolls and then refreshes, and `request` refuses while the wallet is still on its enrolled `pk_0` (`ClientError::EnrolledSerial`, pointing to `tv-cli refresh`); the refresh entry shows a balance-0 commitment whose serial the bank never sees. Running `tv-cli enroll` again after it was interrupted between the two steps resumes it: a wallet still on its enrolled `pk_0` skips the enroll and refreshes (`enroll_freshness.rs`). `p4_issuance_join.rs` plays the bank: it joins `issuance.jsonl` with the `Enroll` and `Refresh` entries and checks that no nonzero balance can be named, and that the same join does name the balance of a payment forged straight to an enrolled `pk_0`.
- **Blast radius of an enclave read.** A reader of enclave memory gets the per-boot result key, the pending channel keys with the owner serial each was attested for (`pk_B` of a payment request, `pk_0` of an enrollment, `pk` of a refresh, all of which the bank also sees), and through them the openings, amounts and next serials of the requests sealed to those keys. It gets no network attribute: no address and no digest of one is ever sent to the enclave, so a read does not tie a serial to an address. A copied channel key stops being usable against payers `MAX_KEY_AGE` after its attestation (attestation freshness, Limitations). Freed heap holds no opening of a request already checked: the `tv-enclave` binary runs on a zeroizing global allocator (`EnclaveAllocator`, `zeroizing-alloc`'s `ZeroAlloc<System>`), so every heap block, including pt-tee's decrypted padded plaintext and the CBOR body of each opening, is wiped before it is freed (`tv-enclave/tests/freed_openings.rs` scans every block freed during a payment check). Dead stack holds none either: `Enclave::check` runs the check in a frame of its own and then overwrites the 64 KiB below its caller's frame with zeros (a check uses less than 16 KiB of stack), so the decoded openings, amounts, next serials and next randomness, the commitment hash state and the decryption buffers on the checking thread's stack are gone before the answer is written (`tv-enclave/tests/dead_stack.rs` plants a marker to prove its scan reads dead stack, then scans 512 KiB below the caller after a payment check). The scrub does not reach CPU registers, kernel socket buffers or the stacks of other threads; no opening is decoded on another thread, and the sealed blobs the kernel buffers are ciphertext. Integrity is unchanged: the result key mints nothing without the bank's own link and nonce (P2), and no state moves without its owner's signature (P1).
- **P5 crash safety.** The enclave keeps no state besides per-boot keys and pending channel keys, so an enclave crash before its answer loses nothing on the bank side and the restarted enclave is re-registered by the bank on its first result (or by `register-enclave`); the client sees `Unavailable`, the payee re-attests (`tv-cli request` without an amount re-emits the pending request under a fresh key) and the payer reseals (`tv-cli reseal`). Inputs are deterministic (same `pk`, `C'`, `m`), so a bank crash after the fsynced append and before the answer is covered by B1: the retry returns the stored answer and no second entry is written. A bank crash after the enclave checked an operation but before its flight became durable loses the staged entry: the client's automatic resend reaches the restarted bank, misses B1, and the enclave refuses it because its channel key is consumed. `BankClient::call` counts the attempts that wrote the request, and a `Refused` answer to a `Receive`, `Enroll` or `Refresh` that was sent more than once is reported as `Unavailable`, the same re-attest and reseal guidance a rolled-back flight gets (`p5_crash.rs` drops the first answer after the enclave consumed the key and checks that the resend reads as `Unavailable` and the re-requested payment settles; `bank.rs` unit tests check that a first-try refusal and a resent `Lookup` stay `Refused`). Tests: `p5_crash.rs`; scripts: `crash-demo.sh`, which injects the two crashes through environment hooks: `TV_CRASH_BEFORE_ANSWER=1` makes `tv-enclave` exit with code 86 after running a `Check` and before writing its answer (honoured only with `--mock`, and only in a binary built with the `test-hooks` cargo feature, which the workspace build of the demo scripts enables through `tv-tests` and the Nitro image build `cargo build -p tv-enclave` does not; without the feature the variable is an error at startup), and `TV_CRASH_AFTER_APPEND=1` makes `tv-bank serve` exit with code 87 after its fsynced append and before its answer (honoured only with `--dev`). Both hooks fire once per matching request and are described in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Bank store

`tv-bank init --data D` builds the two keys, the bank wallet and a `log.jsonl` holding only its format header line `{"log":"flights"}` in a sibling staging directory (`D.init-<pid>`, fsynced) and renames it to `D` (then fsyncs the parent), so an interrupted init leaves no half-initialised `D`; a `D` that exists and is not empty is refused, an empty one is replaced. `Bank::open` (and so `serve`) refuses a data dir missing `state.key`, `issuance.key`, `bank-wallet.json` or `log.jsonl`, and the store opens the log without creating it, so a lost log is never recreated empty (which would make every spent serial spendable again). I/O errors on `log.jsonl` and `issuance.jsonl` name the file.

`log.jsonl` under `--data`: one JSON line per settled operation, written and fsynced before the answer, and each flight (the lines of one group-commit write, below) closed by a trailer line `{"flight":{"len":N,"sha256":"..."}}` that carries the byte length and SHA-256 of the flight's lines and goes out in the same write and fsync. On open the file must start with that exact header line, newline included; an empty log, a cut or damaged header, or a log without one is refused and left untouched (`Corrupt` at line 0), since init writes and fsyncs the header before the rename and only damage can leave it partial, and opening it as empty would make every spent serial spendable again. Replay is anchored at the header, and the file is then replayed into the spent set, the enrolled set, the commitment set, a serial index (spend entries override enroll entries) and a digest index; a flight's entries are applied only once its trailer checks. Flights are written one at a time, each after the previous one's fsync returned, so only the final flight can be torn by a power loss, and the trailer tells it apart from the confirmed prefix. Whatever follows the last valid trailer (or the header, for the first flight) is that final, unconfirmed flight and is truncated when it has the shape of a torn write: it contains zero bytes (an unsynced multi-page append after power loss: the file grew, some pages were never written, later pages, possibly holding whole lines or the trailer, landed), it ends in an unterminated line, or it is made only of complete, decodable entry lines whose `seq` values continue the replayed log without a gap (a write cut at a page boundary that fell right after a newline or right before the trailer, by a kill during a multi-page write, a power loss during page writeback or a crash between a short write and its roll back). Everything else is fatal and the bank refuses to start rather than guess which acknowledged entries exist: damage of any kind (zero bytes, an undecodable line, a trailer whose checksum fails, lines no trailer covers) before a valid trailer, which a dead sector or bit rot inside the durable prefix produces, and, after the last valid trailer, an undecodable line without zero bytes, a failing trailer without zero bytes, or complete lines with no trailer that are not a gapless continuation of the log (a log written before the header and flight trailers existed has no header and is refused, never truncated to nothing) (`flight/tests.rs`). The header is recognised only as the first line; anywhere else it is an undecodable line. The truncation only ever drops a flight whose fsync never returned, so no answered operation is lost (P5). Trailers hold only lengths and hashes of public lines, so they add nothing to the bank's view; they are not entries, so scripts count entries as the lines that start with `{"seq":`. `Lookup(serial)` returns the entry that spent the serial, or the enroll entry if it was enrolled and not yet spent. `LogRange(from, limit)` pages the log (at most 1024 per call). The registered enclave result key lives in `enclave.pk`, written to a temporary file that is fsynced before the rename, with the directory fsynced after.

One operation per digest is in flight at a time: `Bank::claim` re-runs the B1 lookup, then enters the digest into the in-flight set; a request that finds its digest already there waits on a `watch` channel for the holder to leave and loops. The claim is released when the handler returns, settled or not, so a refused operation does not block a later copy. `duplicate_in_flight.rs` sends eight copies of one `Receive` at once against an enclave that answers after a delay and checks that all eight read the single settlement.

Writes are group committed. Under the index lock a settlement is *staged*: the entry is applied to the index (so a concurrent request sees its serials as consumed) and its line is buffered, and the request gets a ticket. The request then waits for durability: the first waiter becomes the leader, takes every buffered line in one flight and writes and fsyncs them in a single call outside the index lock (on the blocking thread pool, in a detached task, so a client that disconnects cannot abandon a flight half way); requests that arrive during the flight stage into the next one. The leader gives up the lead before it publishes the new durable length, so a waiter woken by that publication whose entry is still staged always finds the lead free and flushes the next flight itself instead of sleeping until some later request runs one (`commit.rs` checks the order with a probe that tries the lead the moment the length changes). One fsync therefore covers every operation staged since the previous one, and a lone request still gets its own fsync at once. Nothing is promised before durability: an answer is written only after the ticket's flight landed, `Lookup`, `LogRange` and the B1 answer lookup serve the durable prefix only (a B1 hit on a staged entry waits for its flight), so no client ever learns of an entry that could still be lost.

The store fails closed. If the write or its fsync fails, the file is truncated back to its durable length (and fsynced), the index is rebuilt from the durable prefix, every ticket of the failed flight and of the entries staged during it is marked rolled back (the store records the durable length at which each generation ended, so a ticket confirmed by an earlier flight stays durable when a waiter re-checks it after a later flight failed, `commit.rs`) and those operations are answered `Unavailable`, not `Refused`: the enclave consumed their channel keys, so a plain retry of the same request would be refused by the enclave, and `Unavailable` tells the client to re-attest and reseal (the payee re-emits its request, the payer `reseal`s), after which the operation settles from a consistent state (`bank/tests.rs`). If the truncate or the rebuild fails too, the store is poisoned: every request is refused until the bank restarts and replays the file. Disk and memory therefore never disagree about which serials are durably spent, and `docs/BENCH.md` records what the batching buys.

`tv-bank issue --amount N --to-request request.json` pays a user's request from the bank wallet only when the request asks for exactly `N`: the amount is the operator's statement of what was agreed, not the user's file, and a mismatch is refused before the ledger, the wallet or the offer are touched (`BankError::IssueAmount`). `issuance.jsonl` under `--data` is the bank-private issuance ledger: `tv-bank issue` appends one line (time, amount, payee serial, pay digest) after computing the offer but before the bank wallet commits to it and before the offer file is written. If the ledger cannot be written, no offer exists and the bank wallet keeps no pending payment, so nothing can be minted without a ledger line; the ledger can only over-record (a line whose offer was never written or received), never under-record. The pay digest is the identity of an issuance: before appending, `record_issuance` reads the ledger back, drops a torn tail (each record is its own write of one line, so no trailer is needed: an unterminated last line, or a newline-terminated line with zero bytes and no decodable line after it; truncate and fsync, so the new record starts on its own line) and skips the append when the digest is already recorded, so re-issuing the same request after an enclave crash (`issue` again, which reseals the same offer) leaves one line. Because the ledger can over-record (an issue that failed after its line, or one abandoned with `tv-bank refresh --force`, keeps its line), the total issued is the sum over the ledger lines whose digest appears in `log.jsonl`; a plain sum of the amounts is only an upper bound. `read_issuances` ignores the same torn tail and fails on any other undecodable line (`BankError::LedgerCorrupt`, which names the ledger and the line). The ledger is not part of the public log or of `view_bytes`; the amount of an issuance is the bank's own business as payer. The digest lets an operator reconcile the ledger against the public log (an offer that was never received has no log entry).

## Client

`wallet.json` holds the seed, the bank keys, the current index, the current signed state and at most one pending operation (`Enroll`, `Request`, `Pay`, `Refresh`). Every step that exposes a serial or commits to a next state is saved before the network call, with one exception: `request` attests first and saves its pending record after, because the record carries the channel key and result key that the attestation returns. A `request` that fails between the two leaves no pending record; its serial `pk_B` has been named in an `Attest`, which the bank sees anyway (Deviations), and a plain `refresh` moves the wallet off it like any exposed serial. `request.json` is payee-controlled input: the amount in it is whatever the payee wrote, so `tv-cli pay --amount V` and `tv-cli redeem --amount V` take the agreed amount from the payer and refuse a request for any other amount (`ClientError::PayAmount`) before the offer is built, before the wallet records the pending payment and before any file is written, the same check `tv-bank issue --amount` makes on the bank side (`pay_amount.rs`). `tv-cli reseal` takes no amount: it keeps the amount of the pending payment and refuses a fresh request that asks for another one. `wallet.json`, `request.json`, `offer.json` and `receipt.json` are written atomically (temporary file, fsync, rename, directory fsync). `tv-cli receive` writes the receipt before it advances the wallet: if the receipt cannot be written, the wallet keeps its pending request and the command fails, and the retry gets the stored answer (B1) and completes; a receipt is never lost on a settled payment. `finish` completes a payer-side operation from a receipt or from the log; without a receipt it sends one `Lookup` of the wallet's current serial, which the pending operation already exposed. That `Lookup` is the only bank contact the payer makes in a payment and it ties the payer's socket address to `pk_A` right after settlement; `finish --receipt` makes no bank call, which is why `demo.sh` hands the payee's receipt back to the payer, and the log `Lookup` stays the fallback for a lost receipt. `refresh --force` is the way out of a pending payment or request whose serial was exposed by a refused or abandoned payment: it burns the exposed serial. Every `refresh`, forced or not and whatever is pending, starts with one `Lookup` of the wallet's current serial, which the `Attest` right after names anyway, so a forced refresh that abandons a payment the bank never saw has the same wire shape (`Lookup`, `Attest`, `Refresh`) as a plain one and tells the bank nothing about the abandoned payment (`recover_privacy.rs`). For a forced refresh, the answer decides the outcome: if a pending `Pay` actually settled (same digest), it finishes the payment instead of abandoning it (`Refreshed::FinishedSettledPayment`); if another operation spent the serial, it answers `Behind` and keeps the pending record; and if the refresh itself is refused as `Behind` (a race after the lookup), the pending record is put back, so a forced refresh never erases the record of a settled payment. Without `--force`, `refresh` refuses to replace a pending `Pay` or `Request` and points to the command that completes it (`finish` for a `Pay`, `receive --offer` for a `Request`), `tv-cli recover` and `--force`; any other command blocked by a pending operation names the commands that clear that kind (`finish` or `refresh --force` for a `Pay`, `receive --offer` or `refresh --force` for a `Request`, `enroll` again for an `Enroll`, `refresh` again (or `finish` if it already settled) for a `Refresh`). An `Unavailable` answer names the retry of its own operation the same way: re-attest and reseal for a `Receive`, rerun `enroll` or `refresh` for those (the pending record makes the rerun resume with the same next state), rerun the command for an `Attest`. A refused `Receive` says that it exposed the payee serial and names `refresh --force` to burn it, or `request` plus the payer's `reseal` for a fresh offer; a refused `Refresh` that is not `Behind` says to run `refresh` again (`error.rs`, `refresh_pending.rs`). A payer whose `pay` or `reseal` names another payee serial or amount than its pending payment is told that the payee may have abandoned it and to run `refresh --force`, then `pay` the new request, and a `finish` that finds no log entry for a pending payment names `receive`, `--receipt` and `refresh --force`; a payee whose offer was sealed to an earlier request of its wallet is told to have the payer `reseal` against the latest `request.json`, and one whose offer answers another request is told to use the matching offer or `refresh --force` (`refresh_pending.rs`). A payer who lost a race (the serial was spent by another operation, typically from a rolled-back wallet copy) gets `Behind` from `finish` and from a refused `refresh`, both naming `tv-cli recover`.

`recover` downloads the whole public log with `LogRange` pages and walks it locally from `pk_0`, so the bank never learns which serials belong to one wallet; `Lookup` is never used for recovery. For each spend entry it searches the balance `b'` such that `Com(b', pk_{i+1}, r_{i+1})` is one of the entry's commitments, trying the previous balance first and then outward (`b +- 1, b +- 2, ...` up to `B_MAX`), so a wallet with small payments recovers instantly and any wallet recovers in at most `2 * B_MAX` hashes per step. The bank wallet has no `Enroll` entry: its index 0 state (balance = reserve) is signed at init and kept in `bank-genesis.json` (private, fsynced with the rest of the data dir), and `tv-bank recover` walks the log from that genesis state, trying for each entry whose digest is in `issuance.jsonl` the balance minus the issued amount first and falling back to the same outward search (a redeem moves the balance by at most `B_MAX`). That is the way back for a bank wallet restored from a backup or left behind the log (`bank_wallet_recovery.rs`).

The enclave's test hooks (`leak_memory`, `LeakedMemory`, `boot_with_result_key`) exist only with the `test-hooks` cargo feature, which only `tv-tests` enables; `tv_enclave::TEST_HOOKS` reports the gate and `make test` builds and tests `tv-enclave` alone to exercise the production surface.

## Deviations from SPEC.md, all deliberate

- `BankResponse::Unavailable` exists next to the uniform `Refused`: it means the bank could not complete the operation for a reason of its own (enclave link down, or the log write rolled back) and leaks nothing about the request. It lets the client know when to retry, and the client's message names the retry for the operation it ran (re-attest and reseal for a payment). Every bank-to-enclave call (user `Attest`, `Check`, and the registration attestation of `register-enclave` and of a re-registration) is bounded by `ENCLAVE_CALL_TIMEOUT` (10 s, well under the 60 s connection lifetime): a stalled enclave reads as a link failure, so users get `Unavailable` and `register-enclave` fails instead of hanging (`bank/tests.rs`). A `Check` the bank gave up on may still run when the enclave resumes and consume its channel key; the retry is then refused, which the re-attest and reseal path that `Unavailable` points to already covers.
- The enclave result carries the side kinds (user or bank) so the bank knows which key signs each new state; the result signature covers them.
- The bank's own wallet is created at `init` with a state signed by the issuance key; it is never enrolled through the enclave.
- The enclave manifest contains the result key and both bank keys; `tv-enclave` takes `--state-pk` and `--issuance-pk` (or `TV_STATE_PK`, `TV_ISSUANCE_PK`).
- `register-enclave` is a request to the running bank accepted from loopback peers only; it carries no policy. The bank attests the enclave endpoint it was started with under the trust policy given to `serve` (`--allow-mock` or `--pcr0 HEX`, validated at startup) and persists the result key. When a `Check` result does not verify under the registered key, the bank attests the endpoint again under the same policy before refusing: a restarted enclave of the pinned image is re-registered without an operator action, a result signed by any other key is still refused, and an endpoint whose attestation fails the policy (wrong PCR0, mock off loopback, a manifest with other bank keys) leaves the registered key untouched (`enclave_restart.rs`). Registrations are single-flight: `register` and the re-attestation run under one async lock, a mismatch that finds the registered key already replaced by a concurrent re-attestation checks against that key instead of attesting again, `enclave.pk` is written through a temporary file with a per-process unique name, and a re-attestation that fails for a transient reason (enclave unreachable, I/O) is answered `Unavailable` rather than `Refused`, so concurrent first operations after an enclave restart all settle (`enclave_restart.rs`). A registration attestation names no owner serial (`Attest { owner: None }`): the enclave attests a throwaway channel key and keeps nothing, so neither `register-enclave` nor a re-attestation after a signature mismatch leaves a pending key in the enclave (`register_policy.rs`); a user `Attest` always carries its serial.
- `log.jsonl` holds one line per settled operation plus one trailer line per group-commit flight (Bank store), so that a torn final flight can be told from damage inside the durable prefix.
- The default bank port is 7700 (macOS ControlCenter listens on 7000).
- `Attest` names the serial the channel key is for (`owner`: the payee's `pk_B`, the enrolling `pk_0`, the refreshing `pk`). The enclave binds the key to it and refuses, without consuming the key, any Check whose acting serial differs, so a third party holding `request.json` cannot burn the payee's key with an Enroll or Refresh of its own. The bank therefore sees a serial at attestation time instead of at settlement; an abandoned request shows its serial like a refused payment does.
- The enclave receives no network attribute: `Attest` carries the nonce and the owner serial only (`attest_source.rs` decodes the frame the bank's `TcpLink` writes and checks that its fields are exactly `nonce` and `owner` and that the requester's address appears nowhere in it). The per-source cap of pending attestations lives in the bank's front end (`tv_bank::quota`): the bank keys each attested channel key on the requester's host address (`tv_bank::source_of`; in-process callers use `BANK_SOURCE`), in memory only, and when one address holds more than `MAX_PENDING_KEYS_PER_SOURCE` (256) live keys it sends the enclave `Cancel(channel pk)` for that address's oldest key, so a flood of anonymous attestations from one address only evicts the flooder's own keys. A key leaves the bank's count when the enclave answers a `Check` on it with a result, or after `MAX_KEY_AGE`; a refused `Check` does not free the slot, so blobs that never open cannot be used to attest past the cap. The enclave keeps only the global cap `MAX_PENDING_KEYS` (65536, about 20 MiB of key material); beyond it the oldest key bank-wide is evicted. `attest_flood.rs` floods from 128 addresses and checks that each holds 256 keys at the bank and that an in-flight payment from another address still settles; `attest_source.rs` checks the eviction over the real link codec (one `Cancel` frame for 257 attestations).

## Deploy (not exercised yet)

```
docker build -f deploy/tv-enclave.Dockerfile -t tv-enclave .
nitro-cli build-enclave --docker-uri tv-enclave --output-file tv-enclave.eif
```

The image runs `tv-enclave` with `TV_STATE_PK` and `TV_ISSUANCE_PK` from the environment; the bank then starts with `serve --enclave vsock://CID:5005 --pcr0 <PCR0 from build-enclave>` and `register-enclave` triggers the attestation under that pin.

## Limitations

- A refused or abandoned payment shows its serials (`pk_A`, `pk_B`) and the new commitments to the bank; the client refreshes to burn the exposed serial. A payee whose request was consumed by an aborted payment must re-attest before the payer can reseal.
- A `Refused` answer does not say why. The client distinguishes an enclave restart (result key changed since the request) from a genuine refusal by attesting again; a channel key lost for another reason (expired after 10 minutes, consumed by an earlier attempt) also reads as `Refused` and is fixed by re-attesting. The enclave's refusal takes the same time whichever of E1 to E4 failed: every check is evaluated, one state verification per side under the stated side's key, both owner verifications, both next commitments, and a result is signed and discarded on refusal (`refusal_timing.rs` interleaves ten classes of forged `Receive`, the honest one plus nine refusals that between them make every `Pay` refusal but the channel key the first to fail (`Opening`, `Amount`, `Bounds`, `NextCommitment`, `OwnerSignature`, `SameSerial`, `NextSerial`, each class checked against the enclave's own `check_pay`), and asserts their median enclave times within 8 percent of each other; the measured spread is under 1 percent). Three refusals remain faster and are not padded, because none depends on an opening value: a `Check` naming a channel key the enclave does not hold, a sealed blob that does not open under the key, and a signature that is not even well formed (its `R` is not a curve point, which the Ed25519 library rejects before the scalar multiplication); each tells the bank only that the client sent malformed input.
- Channel key eviction is bounded per source, not per user: `Attest` is unauthenticated, so the only handle on a flooder is the host address the bank's front end sees. An attacker holding more than `MAX_PENDING_KEYS / MAX_PENDING_KEYS_PER_SOURCE` (256) distinct addresses can still fill the enclave's table within the 10-minute key age and evict every in-flight channel key bank-wide (availability only: the payee re-attests, the payer reseals, nothing is minted or lost). Honest users behind one NAT address share that address's quota of 256 pending keys; a consumed key frees its slot at once, so only abandoned requests count.
- Attestation freshness rests on the document's `timestamp`. `verify_bundle` (every attestation a client seals to: enroll, refresh, request, pay) refuses a document `MAX_KEY_AGE` (10 minutes, `tv_core`, the same constant after which the enclave drops a pending key) old or more (`ClientError::StaleAttestation`) or more than `ATTEST_CLOCK_SKEW` (30 s) ahead (`ClientError::FutureAttestation`), so an honest payment loses nothing it had not already lost to the enclave's expiry. Bob's nonce is fresh for Bob only: the check is what stops a payee who colludes with a reader that copied a channel key during a past break from replaying that old attestation to a payer and reading her opening offline. The payer-side exposure of a copied key therefore ends `MAX_KEY_AGE` after its attestation. On Nitro the timestamp is signed under the AWS attestation chain, outside enclave memory; in `--mock` mode it comes from the mock signer in the enclave process, so the check means something only on Nitro. A host-level reader able to forge attestation documents is outside what it can stop. Test: `stale_attestation.rs` re-dates a mock document whose channel key the test holds and shows that `pay` refuses it stale or ahead, and accepts it fresh (the opening it seals then opens with the leaked key).
- The enclave signature is kept as a second check next to the private link (advisor choice). The integrity boundary is the reachability of the enclave endpoint: on Nitro the vsock CID the bank was started with, with `--mock` the loopback port on the bank host. Attestation happens at `register-enclave` and again whenever a result fails to verify under the registered key; a `Check` whose result verifies carries no attestation, so whoever answers at that endpoint with the registered key settles. B3 covers one failure the link alone would not, a link exposed to others while the result key stays private: then a stranger on the link cannot forge a result over the bank's nonce and digest. It does not cover the model's own case of enclave memory being read: a leaked result key signs any nonce, so with a readable enclave B3 adds nothing and only the endpoint's reachability keeps a remote reader from minting. Re-attesting on a signature mismatch lets the bank follow a restarted enclave of the pinned image without an operator action; it does not notice an enclave replaced by another holder of the registered key, since such results verify and trigger no attestation. Attesting on every call would close that gap at the cost of one attestation per operation; it is not implemented.
- The enclave refuses a next serial that collides with a serial of the same operation (`Refusal::NextSerial`), and that is the only check on next serials: the enclave is stateless and the bank never sees a next serial, so nothing stops a modified client from committing its next state to a serial that is already spent, or to another party's live serial, including the bank wallet's. The parked state is unspendable by its creator (the bank refuses a spent serial at B4a; a foreign live serial is spent by its real owner first, whose own payment settles normally, and the parked state is then behind), `tv-cli recover` stops at that entry with an error because none of the entry's commitments opens with the client's next key, and the client needs its seed and a backup of the wallet to go on. Only the client's own balance is burnt (P3 holds for third parties), and only a modified client does it (`tv-cli` derives every serial from a fresh index).
- `recover` cannot tell a pending operation from the seed; it clears any pending state. Enrolling a serial the wallet already holds a funded state on (`pk_i`, `i > 0`) is self-harm the bank cannot detect (it never sees `pk_i` before it is spent): the bank signs a second, zero state on the same serial, and whichever state is spent first consumes the serial. `recover` treats such an Enroll entry on `pk_i` as a no-op and keeps the funded state it reached; only an Enroll of `pk_0` starts a wallet. Balance search is linear in the distance to the previous balance, and recovery downloads the whole log, which is linear in the number of settled operations.
- Nitro is not deployed: `deploy/tv-enclave.Dockerfile` builds the image, the bank's `serve --enclave vsock://CID:PORT --pcr0 HEX` and `register-enclave` are wired but untested on hardware. All runs so far use `--mock` on loopback.
- One request per TCP connection, no TLS between users and the bank: the sealed parts protect the openings, the public parts are public anyway, but an on-path attacker can replay, drop or rewrite requests. Replays are idempotent. A rewritten request can abort the operation it copies: swapping the two sealed blobs of a `Receive`, or resealing an `Enroll` or `Refresh` blob to the public `channel_pk`, still opens under the channel key, so the enclave consumes that key before its checks refuse the copy, and the honest request is then answered `Refused` on its first try. Integrity is unaffected (nothing is appended, the supply is unchanged); the cost is availability: the payee re-attests and the payer reseals. The key stays single-use on first open on purpose, since keeping it after a refusal would let refused `Check`s reuse a sealed channel. TLS between users and the bank is the planned mitigation.
- Address linkage: the bank sees the source address of every connection, so outside the loopback demo one address ties together the `Attest`, `Enroll`, `Receive`, `Lookup` and `Refresh` of one wallet, which the log alone does not. The bank keeps the address out of its routine log lines (only a connection that ends with an error is logged with its peer) and holds it in memory only, next to the pending channel keys it counts for the per-source quota; the enclave never sees it. The linkage still exists at the socket; a mixing or onion transport would be needed to remove it.
